Intersection Heat Field Protocol

Pedestrian heat, shade & intersection observations

Use three Kestrel 5400 Heat Stress Trackers and synchronized pedestrian observations to measure how pedestrian-scale thermal exposure differs across sun, shade, corners, and time.

Field logic: weather station conditions describe the broader atmosphere; paired Kestrels quantify what pedestrians experience locally; corner observations document shade opportunity and behavior.

Core structure

Intersection→Corner→Observation ID→Signal cycle + behavior

Minimum viable output

Thermal

  • Air temperature
  • Globe temperature
  • Relative humidity
  • Wind speed
  • WBGT
  • MRT later, if defensibly derived

Spatial + behavioral

  • Usable dwell area and shaded fraction
  • Shade source and distance to shade
  • Pedestrian arrivals / waiting
  • Sun vs shade occupancy
  • Sun→shade / shade→sun movements
  • Crossing initiation by pedestrian signal phase

Field steps

  1. Before study: label sensors K01–K03, synchronize clocks, use the same units and 1-minute logging interval, then co-locate all three for 30–60 minutes to check offsets.
  2. Before each session: verify time, battery, memory, logging, site ID, corner IDs, and signal timing.
  3. Map the four corners: identify usable dwell area, crosswalk/pushbutton location, tree/building shade, and nearest usable shade.
  4. Deploy Kestrels: preferred setup is K01 representative sun, K02 representative shade, K03 stable nearby reference.
  5. Assign observation IDs: use a sequential ID for every full signal cycle observed, for example O01, O02, O03.
  6. For each ID: record cycle start time, corner, signal phase/cycle identifier, and pedestrian behavior counts.
  7. Context page: write weather, obstruction, crowd surge, construction, unusual signal operation, or other comments under the same observation ID.
  8. Close session: stop logs, export raw Kestrel CSVs, export observations, upload photos/notes, and verify all files open in Box.

Data transfer + Box

  1. Stop Kestrel logging.
  2. Connect through Kestrel LiNK and export the complete raw log as CSV.
  3. Export pedestrian observations separately.
  4. Rename files before upload.
  5. Upload logs, observations, photos, and notes.
  6. Open files from Box once to verify transfer.

Minimal definitions for consistent field coding.

Instructions

Start timeClock time at the start of the full observed signal cycle.
Signal phase IDConsistent code identifying the pedestrian crossing/movement being observed.
Peak waitingMaximum number visibly waiting on the observed corner during that cycle.
Waiting sun / shadeCount at a consistent point near peak queue. Classify by where the pedestrian is standing.

Crossing startsCount pedestrians by signal indication when they begin crossing: WALK, flashing DON'T WALK, or steady DON'T WALK.
Shade fractionApproximate shaded usable waiting area ÷ total usable waiting area at the relevant observation time.
Dwell areaUsable pedestrian waiting space; exclude roadway, planters, inaccessible or blocked areas.
