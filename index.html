Intersection Heat Field Protocol

Pedestrian heat, shade & intersection observations

Use three Kestrel 5400 Heat Stress Trackers and synchronized pedestrian observations to measure how pedestrian-scale thermal exposure differs across sun, shade, corners, and time.

Field logic: weather station conditions describe the broader atmosphere; paired Kestrels quantify what pedestrians experience locally; corner observations document shade opportunity and behavior.

Core structure

Intersection→Corner→Time block→Signal cycle / pedestrians

Minimum viable field output

Thermal

  • Air temperature
  • Globe temperature
  • Relative humidity
  • Wind speed
  • WBGT
  • MRT later, if defensibly derived

Spatial + behavioral

  • Usable dwell area and shaded fraction
  • Shade source and distance to shade
  • Pedestrian arrivals / waiting
  • Sun vs shade occupancy
  • Sun→shade / shade→sun movements
  • Crossing initiation by pedestrian signal phase

Four intersections

Use the same protocol at all four sites. Do not treat an intersection as one thermal condition: each of its four corners can have a different shade geometry and can change materially over the day.

Field steps

  1. Before study: label sensors K01–K03, synchronize clocks, use the same units and 1-minute logging interval, then co-locate all three for 30–60 minutes to check offsets.
  2. Before each session: verify time, battery, memory, logging, site ID, corner IDs, and signal timing.
  3. Map the four corners: identify usable dwell area, crosswalk/pushbutton location, tree/building shade, and nearest usable shade.
  4. Deploy Kestrels: preferred setup is K01 representative sun, K02 representative shade, K03 stable nearby reference. Keep heights comparable and outside pedestrian paths.
  5. Photograph placements: one overview plus each sensor/corner setup. Record whether each sensor is in sun or shade and the shade source.
  6. Start synchronized observation: record environmental conditions automatically and pedestrian/shade/signal observations by time block or signal cycle.
  7. Repeat across time: revisit sites when façade/tree shade has moved enough to change corner exposure.
  8. Close session: stop logs, export raw Kestrel CSVs, export observation data, upload photos/notes, and verify all files open in Box.
Rule: collect broadly and consistently. Derive MRT, sun–shade contrasts, rates, and statistical models later. Do not manually edit RAW files.

Useful derived values later

  • Shade fraction = shaded usable dwell area / total usable dwell area.
  • Shade occupancy rate = pedestrians waiting in shade / pedestrians with shade available.
  • Shade-seeking rate = sun→shade movements / eligible pedestrians.
  • Thermal contrast = sun measurement − shade measurement for Tg, WBGT and MRT.

Data transfer + Box

After every session

  1. Stop Kestrel logging.
  2. Connect through Kestrel LiNK and export the complete raw log as CSV.
  3. Export the pedestrian/field observation file separately.
  4. Rename files before upload.
  5. Upload Kestrel logs, observations, photos, and notes to the session folder.
  6. Open files from Box once to verify transfer.

Session notes

Field Variable Guide

Minimal instructions. Collect consistently; derive metrics later.

Dwell area (m²)Usable pedestrian waiting space only. Exclude roadway, fixed obstructions, planters, and unusable edge space.
Shade fraction (%)Estimate the fraction of usable dwell area shaded at that timestamp. Photograph the corner so the estimate can be checked later.
Shade sourceTree, building façade, engineered structure, mixed, or none. Record the dominant source.
Distance to usable shade (m)Shortest practical pedestrian path from the normal waiting location to shade that can actually be occupied.
Kestrel measurementsLog automatically every 1 minute: air temperature, globe temperature, RH, wind, WBGT and timestamp. Record sensor ID and its sun/shade condition.
Sensor placementKeep K01/K02/K03 at comparable pedestrian-relevant height and outside the travel path. Prefer representative sun, representative shade, and stable reference.
Total waitingCount people waiting on the corner at the same point in each signal cycle or use a consistent fixed time block.
In shade / in sunClassify by the pedestrian's primary body position at the observation instant. Keep ambiguous boundary cases consistent.
Sun→shade / shade→sunCount observable relocations while the person is waiting. Record movement, not presumed motivation.
Crossing phaseCode the pedestrian signal displayed when crossing begins: WALK, flashing DON'T WALK, or steady DON'T WALK.
Context notesOnly note changes likely to matter: clouds, unusual wind, construction, blocked shade, event surge, signal anomaly, sensor disturbance.
PhotosAt minimum: intersection overview plus each sensor/corner setup. Use filenames that include site, date, corner and sequence.

Close-out: export Kestrel CSVs through LiNK; export observation data separately; upload RAW files, photos and notes to the matching Box session folder; verify the files; do not edit RAW.
